## Main scientific objective

Rebuild the study around the following question:

> How do no-slip confinement, operating condition, and three-dimensional wake structure affect POD compactness, basis transferability, and sparse wall-pressure observability?

The new work should contain:

1. A validated two-dimensional WaterLily dataset for a cylinder near a no-slip tank wall.
2. Correct POD analyses that separate fluctuation amplitude, modal compactness, and out-of-condition generalization.
3. A regenerated synthetic wall-pressure/gappy-POD benchmark.
4. A targeted three-dimensional subset using the same solver, geometry, boundary treatment, and cross-sectional resolution as the 2D study, if GPU memory and validation permit it.

## Problems found in the legacy study

### Invalid POD reconstruction

The legacy sensitivity script uses the method of snapshots but forms modes as `Phi = Y @ U` without division by the singular values. The columns are not orthonormal, yet coefficients are computed using `Phi.T @ Y`. Consequently, `Phi @ Phi.T @ Y` is not an orthogonal POD projection.

The legacy mode slice is also off by one: when the first cumulative-energy index reaching 99% is found, the code excludes that mode from the reconstruction.

This error exactly reproduces the very large hard-coded RMSE values used in the manuscript. All legacy sensitivity RMSE figures and conclusions derived from them must be treated as invalid until recomputed.

### Raw RMSE at a separately selected 99% rank is not a representability comparison

For a correct in-sample POD projection retaining 99% of the snapshot variance,

```text
||Y - Y_hat||_F / ||Y||_F ~= sqrt(1 - 0.99) ~= 0.1.
```

Therefore, raw RMSE at a separately selected 99% rank largely follows the fluctuation amplitude of each case. It cannot by itself demonstrate that one flow is intrinsically less representable than another.

### Wrong wall physics

The legacy simple-cylinder data appear to have used the outer LilyPad domain boundary as the tank wall. That boundary sets zero normal velocity and approximately zero tangential gradient, i.e. a free-slip/reflection condition. It does not form the no-slip wall boundary layer assumed in the manuscript's physical interpretation.

The new simulations must use explicit stationary immersed wall bodies whose surface velocity is zero. The default outer WaterLily boundary remains outside/inside the solid wall region and must not be mistaken for the physical tank wall.

### Insufficient legacy spatial resolution

The old baseline used approximately 6.4 cells across the cylinder and only about 2.8 cells across the smallest gap. The nominal doubled grid used approximately 12.8 cells across the cylinder. Merely doubling the old resolution is not sufficient evidence of grid independence.

### Validation leakage in the pressure benchmark

The legacy pressure benchmark randomly assigns individual snapshots from every Re-gap trajectory to training, validation, and test sets. Temporally adjacent states from the same trajectory therefore occur in all subsets. It estimates interpolation of already-observed trajectories, not generalization to an unseen operating condition.

The code also subsamples every sixth selected training snapshot and every fourth validation snapshot without disclosing this in the manuscript.

### Synthetic sensing is not experimental fusion

The eight old sensors were samples from the same CFD pressure fields used to form the POD basis. There was no experimental noise, numerical/experimental model discrepancy, or actual multi-fidelity fusion. The revised paper must call this a synthetic sparse reconstruction or observability benchmark.

## Clarification: experimental-design grid versus simulation grid

When the author refers to reconsidering the "Cartesian grid," they mean the **experimental scenario grid in parameter space**: the selected combinations of Reynolds number and normalized wall gap. They do not mean replacing WaterLily's uniform Cartesian CFD mesh.

Keep a full factorial Re-by-gap design if computationally feasible because it permits clean separation of Re and gap effects. However, the gap coordinates should be nonuniform. Use more scenarios in the small-gap and transition regions and fewer in the clearly large-gap/asymptotic region.

Do not assume the legacy regime thresholds are correct. They were inferred partly from invalid reconstruction curves and from literature involving potentially different wall conditions. The new sampling should be dense enough to discover transitions rather than encode them in advance.

### Candidate two-dimensional parameter matrix

Start from the following candidate Reynolds-number levels:

```text
Re = [1000, 1500, 2000, 3000, 4000, 6000, 8000, 10000]
```

Use a nonuniform candidate gap grid such as:

```text
h/D = [0.20, 0.30, 0.44, 0.60, 0.75, 0.90, 1.10,
       1.30, 1.50, 1.70, 2.10, 2.60, 3.30, 4.20]
```

This gives 8 x 14 = 112 cases, comparable in size to the old 100-case grid, but it reallocates cases toward the physically difficult region.

Treat this list as a candidate, not an immutable instruction. Before finalizing it:

- Ensure the smallest gap has enough cells at the production resolution. Aim for at least 8-12 cells across the smallest physical fluid gap; more may be required by convergence.
- If `h/D=0.20` cannot be resolved at the selected production mesh, either increase resolution or remove that level transparently. Do not simulate a nominal gap that is mostly occupied by the BDIM kernel.
- Preserve at least four large-gap values so the approach to weak confinement can be measured rather than assumed.
- Consider adjusting levels after the convergence/pilot cases show where pressure, force, or spectral transitions actually occur.
- If the full 112-case design is too costly, preserve all small/intermediate gap levels and reduce the number of large-gap levels before reducing small-gap coverage.

## Geometry and boundary conditions

Use nondimensional quantities based on cylinder diameter `D` and incoming speed `U` wherever possible.

The old physical domain was approximately `40D x 20D`, with the cylinder center approximately `11.4D` downstream of the inlet. This is a starting point, not automatically a validated domain.

Required geometry:

1. A circular cylinder of diameter `D`.
2. A stationary lower no-slip wall represented as an explicit immersed solid slab or half-space.
3. Preferably also an explicit stationary upper wall matching the tank geometry, unless a justified far-field/symmetry alternative is adopted.
4. Place the cylinder center at `y = wall_surface + h + D/2`.
5. Keep wall thickness greater than the BDIM kernel support and place the outer computational boundary inside or beyond the solid slab so the default free-slip domain boundary cannot affect the fluid.

The flow simulation body may be a union of the cylinder and walls. Retain separate references to the cylinder and wall geometries so that cylinder forces and wall quantities can be evaluated separately; do not accidentally integrate pressure force over the union when reporting cylinder drag/lift.

### Inlet/outlet choice

The main fixed-cylinder benchmark may use uniform inflow and stationary no-slip walls, but then it is a developing-wall-boundary-layer problem. Quantify the upstream wall boundary layer at the cylinder position.

Use a convective outflow if supported and validate that domain length/outlet position does not affect the reported statistics.

Do not silently substitute a fully developed channel profile. If a developed profile is chosen, document the physical interpretation and regenerate all cases consistently.

A translating-cylinder-in-still-fluid subset can be retained because it is closer to the motivating swimming problem, but do not mix it into the fixed-cylinder parameter study without clear labeling.

## Stage gates

### Gate 1: one validated 2D case

Implement one moderate case, for example `Re=1000`, `h/D=1.3`, with:

- explicit stationary no-slip walls;
- cylinder geometry;
- correct inflow/outflow treatment;
- GPU execution;
- pressure, velocity, force, time, timestep, and mask output;
- checkpoint/restart;
- binary chunked output;
- diagnostic plots of velocity, vorticity, pressure, and wall-normal profiles.

Demonstrate that:

- normal velocity vanishes at the physical wall;
- tangential velocity approaches zero at the wall;
- a wall boundary layer forms;
- cylinder drag/lift and shedding are plausible;
- pressure is well behaved on the fluid side of the immersed wall;
- the run can restart without changing results materially;
- GPU memory and output throughput are known.

Do not proceed to the production matrix until this gate passes.

### Gate 2: spatial, temporal, and domain convergence

Suggested pilot operating points:

```text
Re = [1000, 10000]
h/D = [0.44, 1.70, 4.20]
D/dx = [16, 32, 64]
```

This is an initial 18-run spatial-resolution study. Adjust the grids if GPU memory or the smallest-gap cell count demands it.

For at least a low-Re and a high-Re difficult case, also test:

- a smaller timestep or stricter CFL setting;
- a longer downstream domain or shifted outlet;
- a longer statistical sampling interval.

Compare at minimum:

- mean drag coefficient;
- RMS lift coefficient;
- Strouhal number or dominant shedding frequency;
- mean and RMS wall-pressure distributions;
- wake velocity/vorticity statistics at fixed probe locations;
- fluctuation norms;
- POD eigenvalue spectra and selected modal ranks on an identical physical analysis window.

Define convergence tolerances before selecting the production grid. A provisional target is less than 2-5% change in the quantities central to the paper between the two finest acceptable grids. Report quantities that do not converge.

Do not declare convergence merely because a POD reconstruction error changes modestly.

Present a short convergence report to the user and obtain approval before the full dataset.

### Gate 3: production 2D dataset

After the grid, timestep, domain, transient duration, and sampling interval are validated, generate the approved Re-gap matrix.

Each case must be independently reproducible from a configuration file. Prefer TOML or YAML configuration plus a master case table. Do not hard-code one case at a time in a Julia script.

Required per-case metadata:

- case identifier;
- Re, `h/D`, `D/dx`, `U`, `nu`, BDIM kernel width;
- domain dimensions in cells and in `D`;
- cylinder and wall coordinates;
- inlet/outlet and periodic-boundary settings;
- Julia, WaterLily, CUDA, and Git versions;
- GPU model;
- timestep history or summary;
- warm-up interval;
- snapshot times in convective units `tU/D`;
- random/perturbation seed, if any;
- completion and convergence diagnostics.

### Gate 4: targeted 3D subset

Only start 3D after the 2D setup and production cross-sectional resolution have been validated.

Use an effectively infinite cylinder with periodic spanwise boundary conditions, not a finite cylinder with end effects, unless the scientific question is explicitly changed.

Candidate subset:

```text
Re = [1000, 10000]
h/D = [0.44, 1.30 or 1.70, 4.20]
```

This gives six matched 2D/3D operating points. Add one intermediate Re only if resources allow.

Use a provisional periodic span of at least `Lz = 4D`, which can accommodate the canonical long Mode-A scale and multiple shorter Mode-B wavelengths. Verify that this span is adequate for the selected high-Re cases rather than treating it as proven.

The 3D subset should use the same cross-sectional `dx/D` as the production 2D dataset. If GPU memory prevents that, reduce the physical streamwise/vertical domain only after testing domain independence. Do not quietly use a coarser 3D mesh and call the results directly comparable.

Before the six cases, run one 3D memory/performance pilot and report:

- GPU peak memory;
- wall time per convective unit;
- pressure-solver convergence;
- output volume per snapshot;
- estimated total runtime and storage.

## Simulation duration and snapshot sampling

Define time in convective units `t* = tU/D`.

Separate two datasets conceptually:

1. Startup/transient interval.
2. Statistically stationary or statistically developed interval, where one exists.

Do not combine them without labels. Determine the warm-up duration from drag, lift, wall pressure, and wake probes rather than copying the old nominal duration.

For stationary statistics, sample enough shedding cycles to make force, pressure, and spectral estimates stable. A provisional target is at least 20 statistically developed shedding cycles, but verify convergence of the statistics with window length.

Choose snapshot cadence based on the highest relevant resolved frequency and POD convergence, not on solver timesteps. Store snapshot timestamps explicitly. Avoid saving every CFD step.

## Output and storage design

GPU computation may be fast enough that storage and transfer become the bottleneck. Do not use CSV for full fields.

Use an efficient binary format such as JLD2 or HDF5 with:

- `Float32` fields where validated;
- chunked writes;
- compression where it improves total throughput;
- one case per file or a robust case-group layout;
- explicit shapes, coordinates, masks, and units;
- checksums or completion markers;
- append/restart safety.

Do not hold a complete long simulation in GPU or CPU memory before writing it. Transfer and write snapshots incrementally or in bounded batches.

Estimate total storage before production. If full-domain storage is excessive, define and validate a common physical analysis region that contains the near wake and the relevant wall-pressure region. Store full wall-pressure lines separately even if only a cropped 2D/3D field is saved.

At minimum store:

- velocity components;
- pressure;
- fluid/solid mask or signed distance;
- time;
- drag and lift histories;
- wall-pressure distributions;
- selected wake probes;
- timestep/solver diagnostics.

Vorticity should normally be derived from stored velocity rather than stored separately.

## Pressure and wall-sensor extraction

The old pressure benchmark should be regenerated, not removed.

Pressure inside an immersed solid is not a physical sensor value. Define synthetic sensors at reproducible fluid-side coordinates adjacent to the physical wall surface, using a documented interpolation/extrapolation rule. Test convergence of sensor pressure with grid refinement and sensor offset.

Store the full wall-pressure distribution so alternative sensor layouts can be tested later without rerunning CFD.

Pressure has an arbitrary gauge. Apply a consistent reference-pressure convention before comparing cases or forming global POD bases. Record that convention.

Retain eight uniformly spaced sensors as the hardware-matched baseline, but later compare:

- multiple sensor counts, such as 4, 8, and 16;
- uniform eight-sensor placement;
- a fixed wall-constrained QR/QDEIM-style eight-sensor placement learned only from training data;
- controlled additive noise, provisionally 0%, 1%, and 5% of sensor fluctuation RMS.

Do not describe the synthetic benchmark as true CFD-experiment fusion.

## Common-domain and masking decisions

Changing gap moves the cylinder relative to the wall, so the fluid/solid mask changes between cases. This must be handled deliberately before global POD.

Investigate and document at least these alternatives:

1. Laboratory-frame fields on the common tank grid with solid regions excluded by a consistent mask.
2. Cylinder-centered fields interpolated to a common analysis grid, with the wall position varying.
3. A common-fluid subdomain shared by all cases.
4. A properly formulated masked/weighted POD if case-dependent missing regions must be retained.

Do not allow arbitrary pressure values inside the cylinder or wall to dominate the global POD. Add tests showing that the chosen masking/filling convention does not create artificial modes.

The choice must remain compatible with the intended wall-pressure reconstruction task.

## Correct POD implementation

Build a new tested POD module rather than repairing the legacy script in place.

For a centered snapshot matrix `Y`, an acceptable direct SVD is:

```python
U, s, Vt = np.linalg.svd(Y, full_matrices=False)
eigenvalues = s**2 / (n_snapshots - 1)
coefficients = U.T @ Y
Y_hat_r = U[:, :r] @ coefficients[:r, :]
```

For the snapshot method, normalize modes by the singular values explicitly.

Required unit/invariant tests:

- `Phi.T @ W @ Phi` is the identity within tolerance for the selected inner product `W`.
- Full-rank reconstruction recovers the centered snapshots within numerical tolerance.
- The rank retaining threshold `eta` is `searchsorted(cumsum(lambda)/sum(lambda), eta) + 1`.
- The normalized in-sample Frobenius error agrees with the discarded eigenvalue fraction.
- Direct SVD and snapshot-method results agree on a small synthetic matrix.
- CPU and GPU/streaming implementations agree on a small real case.

Use appropriate physical terminology:

- Velocity POD with cell-volume/mass weighting: kinetic-energy POD.
- Vorticity POD with squared `L2` weighting: enstrophy-like fluctuation content, not kinetic energy.
- Pressure POD: pressure-fluctuation variance, not physical energy.

Consider making velocity POD the primary flow-field analysis, pressure POD the sparse-observability analysis, and vorticity a secondary diagnostic.

## Analysis plan

Decide the reported metrics before inspecting final parameter trends. Exact rank values may be selected after the spectra are available, but do not choose metrics solely because they support the old conclusion.

For every case report:

- fluctuation RMS/norm separately from reconstruction error;
- cumulative POD spectrum;
- rank required for 90%, 95%, and 99% retained variance;
- normalized reconstruction errors at common fixed ranks;
- an effective-dimension measure if useful;
- physical force/pressure/wake statistics.

For global or regime/local bases report:

- complete-case held-out projection error;
- sparse wall-pressure reconstruction error;
- sensor residual;
- condition number of the sensor-to-mode map;
- sensitivity to rank, sensor count, sensor placement, and noise.

Do not infer discrete gap regimes only from POD error curves. Support any regime classification using physical quantities such as mean/RMS forces, wall pressure, shedding frequency, wake topology, or an independently defined clustering procedure.

If discrete local bases are retained, discuss or test continuous alternatives such as basis interpolation, soft membership, or mixture-of-experts weighting near transitions.

## Validation strategy for reconstructed pressure

Do not randomly split snapshots from every trajectory as the primary generalization test.

Use grouped validation in which complete Re-gap trajectories are excluded from basis fitting and hyperparameter selection. A stratified five-fold group split over complete cases is a reasonable default, ensuring that each fold contains small-, intermediate-, and large-gap cases.

Possible additional analyses:

- Leave-one-Re-level-out validation to test interpolation/extrapolation in Reynolds number.
- Leave-one-gap-level-out validation where each local model has sufficient neighboring gap levels.
- Temporally blocked validation with guard intervals longer than the pressure autocorrelation time, clearly labeled as temporal rather than operating-condition generalization.

Keep training, validation, and test roles distinct. Sensor placement and rank/regularization choices must use training/validation data only.

## Three-dimensional analysis goals

The 3D subset is not intended to reproduce every 2D parameter case. It should answer whether enforcing two dimensionality produces an artificially compact and observable wake.

Compare matched 2D and 3D cases using:

- modal spectra and retained-variance ranks;
- fixed-rank normalized errors;
- spanwise correlation/decorrelation length;
- wall-pressure coherence in the spanwise direction;
- full-field versus sparse reconstruction;
- conditioning of the sensor-to-mode map.

Define the 3D sensor geometry carefully. Eight sensors along one spanwise line, eight sensors distributed in both streamwise and spanwise directions, and spanwise-averaged pressure are different observation models. Use the layout closest to the physical experiment and label any alternatives as diagnostics.

Do not claim in advance that 3D must worsen POD or observability. Treat that as a hypothesis to be tested.
