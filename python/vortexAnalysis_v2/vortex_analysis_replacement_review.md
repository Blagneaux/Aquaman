## Agreed analysis scope

- Use pressure-conditioned vortices from the x-deformation variant.
- Use the existing pressure classifier with 95% retained variance and a 0.99
  confidence threshold.
- Link fish-like measured-pressure segments to the corresponding fish-model
  vortices and cylinder-like segments to the corresponding cylinder-model
  vortices.
- Retain vortex-track associations with at least 10 consecutive valid visible
  detections in the associated pressure segment. A physical track can intersect
  more than one sensor-specific pressure segment, so this distinction is made
  explicit in the reported counts.
- Do not balance the fish-like and cylinder-like vortex counts.
- Do not train a second vortex classifier and do not calculate SHAP values.
- Retain area and equivalent-circle radius as separate reported descriptors.
- Merge mean core Q and mean absolute core Q.
- Add track straightness, defined as displacement divided by path length.
- Report pooled vortex-level, equally weighted segment-level and paired
  video-level summaries.
- At the segment level, additionally report vortex count, total vortex-frame
  occupancy and simultaneous visible-vortex count.
- Describe all comparisons as distributional associations within the
  pressure-conditioned subset.

## Clarification about segment-level quantities

Duration, displacement and path length do not need to be added again as new
supporting quantities. They are already calculated for each vortex. At the
segment-informed level, their vortex values will be averaged within each
segment, after which the distribution of segment means will be summarised.

The genuinely additional segment-level quantities are:

- number of unique vortices in the segment
- total vortex-frame occupancy, calculated as the sum of valid detected frames
  across the retained vortices
- mean and maximum number of simultaneously visible retained vortices

Track straightness is a new vortex-level derived descriptor. It can then be
summarised at all three analysis levels.

The next objective was to determine how physical vortex properties differed between the model representations associated with confident measured-pressure classifications. The analysis focused on the x-deformation variant with 95\% of simulated pressure variance retained. At the 0.99 confidence threshold, fish-like measured-pressure segments were linked to vortices from the corresponding fish-shaped simulation and cylinder-like segments were linked to vortices from the corresponding cylinder simulation. Descriptor distributions were first summarised across all retained vortex-track associations. A physical track intersecting more than one sensor-specific pressure segment was represented once in each relevant association. To prevent segments containing many vortices from dominating the comparison, each descriptor was then averaged within each pressure segment and the resulting segment summaries were given equal weight. Vortex count, total vortex-frame occupancy, and mean and maximum simultaneous visible-vortex count were also calculated for each segment. Finally, segment summaries were averaged within each source video and model class. Paired cylinder-minus-fish differences were calculated for videos represented in both groups. At each level, sample counts, medians and interquartile ranges were reported. Further details are provided in supplementary section S8.

#### Current text

```latex
Individual-vortex analysis associated fish-like pressure classifications more strongly with trajectory geometry, spatial organisation and persistence than with raw intensity. The distance-filter sensitivity adds context to this pattern. Reducing the exclusion distance admitted more near-body structures and increased the cylinder-like fraction, whereas stronger exclusion generally increased the fish-like fraction. One possible explanation is that the cylinder reproduced some immediate, short-lived disturbances associated with body passage. Structures that persisted and travelled farther from the body were more consistent with the deforming fish model. The change was not strictly monotonic for every class and model, so distance should be treated as a practical proxy rather than a direct measurement of vortex persistence.

This distinction is biologically relevant because a following fish encounters the wake after the generating fish has moved on. Persistent vortices can provide a longer-lived hydrodynamic signal than instantaneous near-body pressure fluctuations. This interpretation is compatible with work on schooling and vortex exploitation \cite{liao2003}, but whether conspecifics use the specific pressure differences identified here remains an open behavioural question.

Fine-scale deformation may vary with swimming style, behavioural state, acceleration, manoeuvring, body roll or morphology, and could consequently alter downstream pressure signatures. This raises the possibility that wakes contain information about individual identity or intent. Neither possibility was tested or demonstrated here. Direct behavioural observations of followers combined with pressure measurements would be required to determine whether fish detect or use such variation.

At a 99\% confidence threshold, 63 fish-labelled and 63 cylinder-labelled vortices were available for the second-stage training set. The final hyperparameters were a learning rate of 0.0069, weight decay of $2 \times 10^{-6}$, 75 training epochs and a batch size of 8.
